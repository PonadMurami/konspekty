Goal of the meeting and introduction for the animator
=====================================================

The goal of this meeting is to find or start finding "elements of oneself" that make up personal spirituality - the charism of life. It is important that at the end of the meeting everyone leaves with the awareness that God has placed in them their unique and unrepeatable vocation and only they themselves can discover and realize it in themselves.

A large role in this meeting should be played by the animator's testimony about the personal path to faith. It is not about some spectacular miracles, but more about experiencing everyday life in faith and about individual elements of spirituality.

There are many questions and exercises in the outline. The animator's task is not to "realize the outline" to fit everything at all costs, but to choose those questions which according to him are most important for the group.

St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein)
    was born in 1891 in Wrocław as the eleventh child of deeply believing Jews. Although the rules of religion were zealously observed at home, Edith at the age of 20 considered herself an atheist. In 1911 she passed her maturity exam with excellent results and undertook philosophical studies in Wrocław. Two years later she went to Göttingen, and began writing her doctorate. Work on it was interrupted by the outbreak of World War I. Edith volunteered for the Red Cross, became a nurse and began helping those with infectious diseases. After half a year of work, completely exhausted, she was released from sanitary service. In 1917 she obtained the title of doctor of philosophy. In 1921 her conversion took place thanks to encountering the autobiography of the mystic and doctor of the Church, St. Teresa of Avila. She then asked for baptism and on January 1, 1922 received baptism and First Holy Communion. In 1934 she received the Carmelite habit. After the novitiate she took religious vows and took the name Benedicta of the Cross. At that time National Socialism covered the whole of Germany with its range. Teresa, realizing that her Jewish origin could pose a threat to the monastery, moved to Echt in the Netherlands. During the mass arrest of Jews she was arrested by the Gestapo and interned. Then she was deported to the camp in Auschwitz, where she died in 1942.

John Paul II (Karol Wojtyła)
    was born in Wadowice. Already in childhood he was involved in the life of the parish, he performed the duties of an altar server with dedication. He began his education in the gymnasium in Wadowice, in 1938 he undertook Polish studies at the Jagiellonian University, where he showed great activity – he took part in literary meetings, participated in the preparation of performances, wrote his first poetic works. In 1942 Wojtyła decided to study theology in the secret Metropolitan Seminary in Kraków. In 1946 he received priestly ordination. The following years in Karol Wojtyła's biography are already a period of intensive theological work, cooperation with Catholic magazines (including "Tygodnik Powszechny") and climbing subsequent rungs in the church hierarchy. In 1958 he was appointed bishop, in 1963 archbishop, and in 1967 he received the cardinal nomination. After the death of John Paul I in 1978, the second conclave elected Karol Wojtyła as the new pope. The Pole took the name John Paul II, and his pontificate lasted over 26 years and was one of the longest in history. The Polish pope also had a great influence on the changes that took place in Europe at that time, including the fall of communism. The Polish pope was known as a great supporter of ecumenism and a pilgrim – he made 104 pilgrimages (visiting 134 countries around the world), including as many as 8 to Poland. He died on April 2, 2005.

Faustina Kowalska (Helena Kowalska)
    was born in 1905 in Głogowiec. Saint of the Catholic Church, proclaimer of the cult of Divine Mercy. She was born in a large family, her father Stanisław Kowalski and mother Marianna née Babel, were farmers. She started school in Świnice Warckie, but did not even finish three classes. A desire appeared in her to give her life to God by going to a convent, which met with categorical opposition from her parents. In 1924, without their consent, she went to Warsaw and joined the Congregation of the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy, taking the religious name Faustina. At the persuasion of her confessor Father Michał Sopoćko, Faustina began to keep a detailed record of her experiences, which had the character of revelations, thanks to which the image of Jesus I Trust in You was created and the feast of Divine Mercy was established. She died in 1938.

Francis
    was born into the family of a wealthy merchant Pietro Bernardone. His mother gave him the name John at baptism, which he later changed to Francesco. He attended the parish school at the church of Saint George — where he obtained basic education. He participated in the war between Assisi and Perugia, as a result of which he was imprisoned in Perugia for betraying a comrade. He was released in 1204 due to serious illness. In the church of San Damiano he heard the voice of Jesus Christ speaking to him from the cross. He decided to live in penance; he restored the chapels: San Damiano, San Pietro and the chapel of Our Lady of the Angels in Portiuncula near Assisi. He led a life in solitude. He also helped lepers. He gathered companions with whom he preached ideas of love, purity and poverty. He died on October 3, 1226.

Stanislaus Kostka
    was born probably in December 1550 in Rostkowo near Przasnysz. When he turned 14, together with his brother he was sent to a Jesuit school in Vienna. A year later he fell seriously ill. St. Barbara appeared to him then with two angels. The saint brought him Holy Communion. In the second vision he saw the Mother of God, who places the Child Jesus in his hands. The next morning Stanislaus Kostka got up completely healthy. Parents did not allow the boy to join the Jesuit order. Then Stanislaus Kostka left Vienna in hiding. His older brother set off in pursuit of him. Stanislaus reached Bavaria, more than 600 km away from Vienna. From there Peter Canisius sent him to Rome. He was accepted into the novitiate. At the age of 18 Stanislaus Kostka took religious vows. In the same year he died of malaria.

Mother Teresa of Calcutta
    actually Ganxhe Agnes Bojaxhin, was born in 1910 in Skopje, then Yugoslavia, today Macedonia. Her mother raised her in the Catholic faith. Agnes from early years was fascinated by the lives of missionaries and their ministry. At the age of twelve she decided to dedicate her life to God. At the age of 18 she decided to become a missionary and joined the Institute of the Blessed Virgin Mary. She took religious vows on May 24, 1931, taking the name Teresa. In the second half of the 40s she founded the Congregation of the Missionaries of Charity, which in 1956 obtained the status of an order. It dealt with helping the blind, lepers, disabled, incurably ill. In India and many other countries, thanks to Mother Teresa's initiative, about 50 homes for abandoned children were established, and in 1985 in the USA the first center in the history of the world for AIDS patients was established. She died in September 1997.

Jerzy Popiełuszko
    was born on September 14, 1947 in the village of Okopy near Suchowola in a family of farmers. He was an altar server and stood out for his deep religiosity. He received First Holy Communion and confirmation in the same year – 1956. In the years 1961–1965 he attended high school in Suchowola. He was diligent, but studied averagely. Because of his religiosity, his parents were summoned to school. After graduating from high school, he discovered a vocation in himself, and entered the Higher Theological Seminary in Warsaw. In the 70s he performed pastoral ministry in parishes; Holy Trinity in Ząbki and Our Lady Queen of Poland in Anin and Infant Jesus in Żoliborz. From 1980 he was associated with the social movement "Solidarity", in which he served as chaplain. Fr. Popiełuszko's activity did not please the then authorities, he was repeatedly interrogated, received many letters with threats. On October 19, 1984, uniformed militia officers stopped the car in which Father Jerzy was traveling, and kidnapped him along with his personal driver, who eventually managed to escape. On October 30, the body of the priest was fished out of the Vistula Lagoon near Włocławek. In December 2009 Pope Benedict XVI signed a document stating the martyrdom of Fr. Jerzy, and in June 2010 a beatification mass took place.

Karolina Kózka
    was born in the village of Wał-Ruda near Tarnów in 1898. Her parents owned a small farm. She worked with them on the land. She grew up in an atmosphere of living and authentic faith, which was expressed in common family prayer in the evening and at meals, in daily singing of the Little Office, frequent reception of sacraments and participation in Mass also on weekdays. Their poor cottage was called a "little church". Relatives and neighbors often gathered there for common reading of the Holy Scripture and religious magazines. Her uncle, very religious and involved in apostolic and social activity, had a great influence on Karolina's spiritual development. In her parish she was a member of the Temperance Society and the Apostleship of Prayer and the Archconfraternity of Perpetual Adoration of the Blessed Sacrament. She died in the 17th year of life on November 18, 1914, at the beginning of World War I. A Tsarist soldier abducted her by force and bestially murdered her when she defended herself desiring to preserve virginity. After several days, on December 4, 1914, her body was found in a nearby forest.

* What connects these people? How do they differ?

* Why do we remember them? Why are they worth remembering?

* What does this give us? Are we to be the same?

* What connects us with them?

Each of these people is a known saint/blessed. They reached holiness each in their own way. Their paths were completely different, but the Church recognized each equally as a model to follow. Each of them had to start from something, even they started their path from learning the basic truths of faith, and only later under the influence of their experiences, own reflections and the influence of other people, they themselves began to consciously shape their spirituality.

The beginning of every path is common to all, each of us starts learning from the same things. One cannot specialize in any field without knowing the basics of the Polish language or mathematics. Every child in kindergarten and initial grades of primary school learns the same thing in the same scope, basic skills such as reading, writing, counting, are common to all. In faith it is the same. We start by learning commandments and most known prayers by heart, not from contemplative prayer. **However, one cannot stop at this stage!**

* Which of the learned "basic" elements of faith do I currently consider most crucial for my life? Who and when passed them on to me?

People differ from each other, so it is impossible that the spirituality of each of us will be the same. Developing one's faith, at a certain moment of one's faith each of us discovers ever new elements and aspects of it.

Let's imagine what our spiritual life would look like if we used ALL the proposals offered to us by the Church. We wouldn't have a single day off, because every day there would be a different meeting or service. We would always have a breviary, rosary, scapular, and several different types of prayer books with us. Such religious life would be not only tiring, but above all harmful, that is why it is important to discover and choose something of one's own, one's **charism of life**.

.. Note:: To the animator! Of course, the matter must be put clearly: I cannot resign from the Eucharist, but I can choose whether I prefer the Way of the Cross, rosary, adoration, or breviary. I cannot resign from the teaching of the Church, but I can choose whether on YouTube I listen to Szustak or Jesuits. Or I don't listen at all, just read encyclicals.

Charism of life
===============

**Magnanimous gift**

Let us consider what a charism is? Let's read the passage:

    Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are varieties of service, but the same Lord; and there are varieties of activities, but it is the same God who empowers them all in everyone. To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good. All these are empowered by one and the same Spirit, who apportions to each one individually as he wills.

    -- 1 Cor 12:4-7.11

Let's ask:

* What is this "common good"?

* Since the Holy Spirit acts as He wills and gives everyone different gifts, what influence do we have on what and how we will do in life?

The noun "charisma" comes from the verb charidzomai, which means: to do something pleasant for someone; to do a favor; to give, offer something willingly, kindly, joyfully. The ending -ma expresses the product, effect of this action. Hence charism means "magnanimous gift", "present".

Let's list the types of charisms we know (let's not enter into conversation here, let's treat it as an introduction by the animator to the concept of "charism of life").

* general charisms - e.g. gift of salvation, universal call to holiness
* special charisms - gift of prophecy, gift of tongues, gift of healing, etc.
* religious charism - "Religious charism is what makes a given order itself, and not another order".
* charism of life - analogously: what makes me be myself in faith, and not someone else. :)

**Faith is very individual**. Not in the sense that we are to keep it to ourselves and show it to no one and tell no one about it, but that it is a personal relationship with another Person, and there are no two relationships in the world that would look the same. I will not read "my" faith in a book, I will not copy it from other people, regardless of how holy they might be. I must discover myself what is closest to me, who I am before God.

Faith is not to limit, impose commands and prohibitions, nor is it to make man unhappy, force him to act against himself. Spirituality and vocation are so that man feels completely happy and fulfilled, realizes himself living in accordance with God and His commandments. Fulfills God's plan for himself, creatively using everything that God has placed in his heart. Not because "one has to", but because he wants to, because he knew Love so great that all good deeds flow from It.

**Let us be perfect!**

Let's read the passage:

    I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called, But grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ's gift. Therefore it says, "When he ascended on high he led a host of captives, and he gave gifts to men." (In saying, "He ascended," what does it mean but that he had also descended into the lower regions, the earth? He who descended is the one who also ascended far above all the heavens, that he might fill all things.) And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ, until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to mature manhood, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes. Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ, from whom the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love.

    -- Eph 4:1.7-16

Let's ask:

* What does it mean "to walk in a manner worthy of the calling"?

* [It is about], so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes.

* What calls for myself do I read in this?

Let's read a fragment of the apostolic exhortation of St. John Paul II Christifideles laici.

    We can conclude by quoting a beautiful text of St. Francis de Sales, a great promoter of lay spirituality. Speaking of devotion, that is, of striving for Christian perfection or of "life according to the Spirit", he presents in simple words the vocation of all Christians to holiness, and at the same time specific forms of realization of this vocation: "God creating the world, commanded plants to bear fruit, each «according to its kind» (Gen 1:11). Similarly, he commands Christians, who are living plants of his Church, to bear fruits of devotion appropriate to the state and vocation of each. A nobleman is to practice devotion differently, a craftsman or servant differently, a prince differently, a widow, maiden or married woman differently. And that is not enough. It is also necessary that each individual adapt the way of practicing devotion to his strength, activities and duties (...). It is an error contrary to faith, downright heresy, to want to banish devout life from the soldier's camp, from the craftsman's workshop, from the court of princes, from married life. I admit, Philothea, that for purely contemplative, monastic and religious devotion there is no place in such vocations, however, apart from these types of devotion there are others, capable of perfecting people living in secular professions (...). Wherever we are, we can and should strive for a perfect life".

    -- John Paul II, Apostolic Exhortation Christifideles laici

Let's ask:

* What is devotion according to the author of the text?

* What is vocation according to the author of the text?

* "It is also necessary that each individual adapt the way of practicing devotion to his strength, activities and duties".
    * What is this supposed to consist of in today's times?

    * What does this mean for me?

According to St. Paul, the author of the Letter to the Ephesians, the goal of growing in faith is: "until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to mature manhood, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ". Francis de Sales speaks similarly, identifying devotion with striving for perfection: "we can and should strive for a perfect life". The Evangelist Matthew says in turn: "You therefore must be perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect" (Mt 5:48).

One cannot be satisfied with half-measures, one cannot take the easy way out. To achieve something, to reach happiness, our effort and commitment are needed. In the text above, the author encourages constant development in faith, constant searching and going into the deep to the measure of one's capabilities and "appropriate to the state and vocation of each".

**My place in faith**

Charism of life is a present placed by God in me for others. It is not to be wasted or kept only for oneself. It consists of many small elements, gifts from God, character traits, skills, desires, which only combined together give a full picture.

Below are prepared a few questions that can bring this closer to us. There will be no time to share all of them, so we propose that each person in the group choose 2-3 and share them. The rest can be read and left for reflection at the evening prayer.

* What is my approach to faith? (intellectual, emotional, contemplative)
* What spiritual experiences are close to me? (silent adoration, community prayer with singing, parish service)
* What could be my life motto? It can be a quote from the Holy Scripture, some quote or motto composed by oneself.
* How do I make decisions? How much time does it take me?
* What kind of person do I want to be in 5 years? What kind was I 5 years ago? What is changing in me?
* What gifts, skills, talents do I have?
* Who inspires me? What kind of people am I drawn to?
* What gives me the most joy in life?
* What is most important in life according to me?
* What are my desires regarding my future?

Summary
=======

It is worth having courage and saying: "I am who I am. I have specific gifts and deficiencies, flaws and advantages, desires and aversions, which make me unique and unrepeatable. The Pope/priest/mom/grandma/animator inspire me, I admire them, but I don't want to be the same as them. I want to be myself before God!"
