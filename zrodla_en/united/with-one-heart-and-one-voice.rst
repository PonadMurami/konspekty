Goal of the meeting
===================

This is not only the first group meeting of this retreat, but for most, it will likely be the first online meeting of this type (both for animators and participants), and for some, it will be the first small group experience ever. For this reason, it is especially important to ensure a sense of safety by establishing appropriate rules. Through this, to a large extent, the meeting aims to bond the small group community. The outline does not contain complicated content to convey; it consists mainly of sharing questions and tips, includes an activation in the form of creating a signature for the unity letter and sending it by email, and at the end, there is time dedicated to very important common prayer.

Meeting goals:
    - presenting the rules of group meetings
    - getting to know the participants and bonding the small group community
    - creating signatures needed for the unity letter
    - common prayer - **let's ensure there is time for it!**

Introduction of rules
=====================

After making sure that all participants have "arrived" at the meeting and technical conditions allow for conducting it, let's proceed to present the necessary rules that will help us create a safe atmosphere and space for sharing and prayer:

- We ensure confidentiality and anonymity of what other group members share about themselves
- We do not take screenshots, we do not record meetings
- We encourage turning on the camera, but we do not insist on it
- Depending on the meeting conditions (noise) and type of equipment (feedback), we mute the microphone when we are not speaking (Ctrl + D)
- We try not to interrupt each other - in online conditions this can be difficult, but it is important not to do it intentionally
- We listen to each other, especially since the meetings are mainly of a sharing nature!
- We do not comment unnecessarily, we do not judge others' statements
- We share our own feelings, experiences, we do not generalize or moralize
- We respect each other and our time

We can ask if these rules are clear to everyone and if the participants agree to them, and if they have anything else to add. After the final agreement, we proceed to getting to know each other.

Integration + sharing
=====================

We propose introducing ourselves according to the following key:

- My name is...
- I am from...
- I deal with...
- I want you to know about me that I like...
- From today's conference, I remembered most that...
- 3 situations in which I feel the greatest unity with others are...

The next person refers to something they have in common with the predecessor - the animator starts first, while the participant who caught that they have something in common with them shares next, and so on - thanks to this, a dynamic of sharing will be created, because you will have to "seize the opportunity" that I can refer to something. It may also happen that someone will be somewhat forced to search for some similarity, but this will also show that something in common can be found with everyone.

Unity Letters
=============

When everyone has introduced themselves, we proceed to read the unity letter mentioned in the conference. Then we ask participants to sign their name on prepared white sheets of paper (**important: only the first name**), take a photo (preferably with flash) and send it to the email spotkajmysie@ponadmurami.pl (this is not mandatory, let's encourage it, but let's not put pressure that it must be done).

What do letters mean to us?
===========================

When everyone who wanted to has already sent their signatures, we want to reflect on the issue of writing letters in general:

* Why are letters written? What is unique about this form of messaging?

* What was the most important letter in my life?

* What letter would I like to receive someday?

Prayer with the Word of God
===========================

Sometimes it is said that the Bible is one great letter written by God to man. Therefore, we want to lean over selected fragments of St. Paul's letters to various communities and pray with the *echo of the Word*, which consists of reading aloud those verses that touch me the most and which I want to make my own prayer.

It is very possible that for many such prayer may be a novelty (and probably for almost everyone it will be a novelty to pray this way online). For this reason, at this first meeting, we want to get a little familiar with this prayer, giving it a slightly simplified form:

- at the beginning, the animator reads all fragments aloud;
- then we give ourselves a moment to look at them in silence; everyone chooses one fragment that speaks to them the most, and with which they would like to pray
- at the end, everyone reads their chosen fragment aloud in turn, after which the animator ends with a short prayer (it can simply be "Glory be to the Father") and we all end together with a common "Amen".

Practical notes: It is worth inviting participants to adopt a comfortable but appropriate posture for prayer (they can still sit in front of the computer/phone, but let them e.g. straighten up). It can also be mentioned that if someone feels silly praying "to the screen", they can place a cross or a holy picture next to it, or focus their eyes on the words of the Holy Scripture. The animator can initiate a few words of prayer from themselves at the beginning, e.g. to the Holy Spirit. Let's not be discouraged if there is a prolonged silence, which in online form can be even more awkward. Let's adjust the prayer time to the level of engagement of the participants.
