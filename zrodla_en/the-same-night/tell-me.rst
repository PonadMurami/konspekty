Meeting Goal
============

This is the first meeting, so let's try to get to know each other and form a small group community. The meeting is placed between the conference and the evening prayer, which is not without influence on the structure of the meeting. Let's try to start by sharing what appeared in us after hearing the conference at the start. Let's remember that participants came to us from all over Poland, so it is important to emphasize the element of building a community.

Community Formation
===================

A moment ago in the conference, we heard that we were created to tell stories. Every story begins with the introduction of the hero, presenting their features and distinguishing elements, starting with the name.

It is worth mentioning that in Jewish culture, a name is not only an element given at birth, but often had a prophetic or spiritual character, because it very often referred to various blessings from God. Moreover, using one's name emphasized that its bearer is a free man.

Let's start forming a small group by talking about our names.

.. warning:: The question regarding the meaning of the name is one of the auxiliary questions, it should not be made the center of this element.

- Do you know the meaning of your name?
- What is your middle name?
- Can you give an interesting story related to your name?

Multidimensionality of the Story (Workshop)
===========================================

If we have the opportunity to see more than one perspective of a story, the story becomes not only closer to us, but it is much easier for us to identify with it, because we are not afraid of what we know very well. In this element, we want to take into the workshop the biblical descriptions of the institution of the Eucharist, which were written not only by eyewitnesses (Matthew) but also by people who did not directly participate in the events in the Upper Room (Mark, Luke, and Paul).

Immediately after reading all fragments, we can ask participants for "hot impressions". Below are sample auxiliary questions:

- Do we have the impression after reading the descriptions that we are dealing with four different descriptions? Or maybe it all sounds the same?
- Let's remember that the authors wrote individual gospels and letters to different communities and ethnic groups. What would I pay attention to if I had to tell the institution of the Eucharist to my parents today? Friends? A newly met person?

.. note:: We can now proceed to the practical part of the workshop. In the attachment to the outline there are all the fragments we just read - we give everyone their own copy.

Participants individually mark on them elements that are common to all descriptions (contrary to appearances, there are fewer of them than you think) and pay attention to elements that are unique. We give participants about 7 minutes to note and think.

Then sharing follows. Auxiliary questions that are worth using to moderate the discussion conducted around the descriptions:

- Which description of the institution of the Eucharist is closest to me? Why?
- "Poured out for many", "poured out for you". What does this mean to me?
- Do I have experience of hearing one story from several perspectives? Does the first "version" mark my reception of subsequent perspectives?
- Let's imagine that after Jesus' death only one description of the institution of the Eucharist was created. What would Christianity look like 2000 years after this fact?

Intimacy of the Story
=====================

We see for ourselves that when we pass on a story, we add a certain unique, intimate character to it. We choose elements that are closer to us, touch our hearts more strongly, or are simply better and more expressive when it comes to emphasizing the meaning of what happened.

We also deal with the second aspect of the intimacy of the story - the story will exert a specific influence on us not only because of what happened, but also through external circumstances - our current emotions, circumstances, life situation. Based on quotes from the Holy Scripture, which are at the end of the outline, let's look at this individual character of the read fragments. Below are auxiliary questions.

Sharing the Story
=================

During the conference, we said that we were created to tell stories. For a story to happen at all, we must have contact with another human being with whom we will be able to share the story and the emotions we just discussed. In our group there are people from different parts of Poland. We can live very far from each other, and yet we will have a lot in common and at some moments we will understand each other perfectly, although we will not say much at all. Some stories we will share with all Poles, some with people from our own region, and some with people from the immediate family. Let's use this element for a joyful end of the meeting, let's share slogans/texts/motifs that are obvious to us although we met only 45 minutes ago.

- If you had to give three sentences that are obvious to Poles and we understand them without additional explanation, what would it be? (Hints for the animator: "call me when Małysz is jumping", "it was like that under communism", "nothing happened, Poles, nothing happened!", "Lithuania, my fatherland".
- What slogans/texts/motifs do you share among your own family/immediate surroundings? Give examples.

Application
===========

We propose, as part of forming a community during our common weekend, to approach three people unknown to us before and ask e.g. about slogans that fell during the previous element of the group meeting. Let's form a community, after all, so much connects us!
