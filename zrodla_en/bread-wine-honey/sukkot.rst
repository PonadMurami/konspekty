Introduction for the facilitator
================================

The first group meeting serves an introductory and integrating function. We would like the meeting to allow experiencing and summarizing the first day of the retreat. In the first meeting we would like to place emphasis on HOSPITALITY. Hospitality understood as the next step after openness and going out to others. It is us who are to give a hospitable space, to which we invite others, so that they can go out to people.

**The meeting requires preparation of materials (At the end of the outline - for self-printing on one's own and distributed on site). Do not forget them.**

Introduction
============

We start with prayer - form according to the animator's discretion, with particular emphasis placed on the request for openness to another human being. It is about readiness to accept and host them in one's space as they are – with their traits, talents and perspective. Jesus teaches us this, confronting people with a way of thinking different than the one they knew and to which they were used.

If there are people in the group without experience in sharing meetings, the animator briefly explains their idea and discusses basic rules.

We start with greeting and getting to know each other.

- We ask everyone for a short introduction. Say a few sentences about yourself, have you been to the Ponad Murami retreat before.

Then we share impressions from the conference.

- What contents from the conference resonate most with what has been happening lately in my life?
- What moved me?

Then we immediately move to the workshop part.

.. warning:: It is important to stick to individual times, because only then will it be possible to realize the full workshop. We realize that this requires concentration on time. It is good to turn on a timer for each exercise so that participants see how much time they have until the end of the task.

The workshop aims to help participants experience what openness to another human being and diversity of people around us is (together we create a group, retreat space and Church, we meet there TOGETHER). The workshop teaches participants openness and consent to diversity around us, to other people and to creative, often unexpected action of God in our life through other people. It is worth paying attention to:

#. *Experience of change* – Participants will experience on their own skin what it is like when someone interferes with their "work". This can evoke different emotions, which is an excellent starting point for reflection on openness.

#. *Work on control and attachment* – Teaches flexibility and the ability to let go of control over something they created.

#. Participants will see how others can enrich their work or change it, which shows that openness can lead to new, unexpected results.

#. *Interaction and building community* – Creates a space for conversation, sharing impressions and getting to know each other better.

Challenges and possible difficulties:

#. *Emotional reaction* – Some may feel frustration if their figurine is changed in a way they do not like.
#. *Risk of lack of engagement* – If someone does not identify with the message of the exercise, they may treat it superficially.
#. *Adaptation to the group* – Depending on the group, it is worth adapting the reflection after the exercise.

Workshop
========

.. note:: The animator puts out a box with pieces of technical plasticine (set released before the meeting). They do not show the box with additional decorations.

**Short introduction** - Explain that it is about the experience of openness, not judging or criticism.
Everyone takes a piece of plasticine, one piece is left so that one can take more in subsequent stages.

**Instruction:**

#. Distribute soft plasticine to each participant. They have 3 minutes to create a figurine – an animal or favorite thing. They can give their work names.

#. After three minutes ask why they created such a figurine (we have about 5 minutes for this). Participants are after introductions, probably they paid attention to various contents from today, finally our figurines are certainly quite different. Summarize briefly the current part of the workshop. Pay attention to the fact that each of the participants had a different idea for the figurine, just as we come from different environments and are at different stages in life. However, we meet here together to mutually draw from each other - similarly to Jews in Sukkot.

#. Then we move to the next part of the workshop. Take out the box with additional elements (decorations etc.).

#. Ask participants to pass their work to the person on the left. The new creator has 2 minutes to introduce any change - with the proviso that the change must add something (one cannot completely remake the figurine).

#. Repeat this once more - the figurine passes to the next person, who again has 2 minutes for modification - again the same rule regarding change.

#. At the end figurines return to their original creators. Each person has an additional 2 minutes to undo or add some modifications.

Reflection and summary
======================

Let's ask ourselves questions:

- What did you feel when someone changed Your work?

- What feelings accompanied You when you gave your figurine to others? (curiosity, fear, reluctance to give it away, ...)

- Did the possibility of seeing ideas of other people (for Your figurine, but also figurines of other people) give You something?

In this exercise you gave space to other people to open up and show their perspectives by modifying your figurine. You invited them to yourselves and opened up to them co-creating your space (this is different from the situation "you can enter, but do not touch anything"). You yourselves also co-created the space of others and shared a part of your skills to show your view. This is the realization of the principle "Who visits me creates me".

Emphasize that our life and plans can be changed – by people, by circumstances, but also by God. HOSPITALITY is the ability to accept others with trust and openness. It is acceptance and accompanying the other in the stage of life they are in. Without interference, rejoicing in what this person experiences, allowing it and remembering this time in oneself. For example, one should not extinguish childlike faith, although with time this faith will become much more mature - it is a stage.

HOSPITALITY does not mean accepting everything thoughtlessly, but the ability to listen, learn and be ready for new experiences. Just like with a guest - not every behavior of a guest (for example aggression) must be accepted by us.

    He who receives you receives Me, and he who receives Me receives Him who sent Me.

    -- Mt 10:40

Hospitality as wisdom of the heart
==================================

Let us read:

    He who has no experience knows little, but he who has traveled has increased his prudence.

    -- Sir 34:10-11

- Did you have a travel experience in your life that in some perhaps smaller, perhaps larger degree changed You and Your way of perceiving reality?

Travels educate, but what educates in them most are meetings with other people, not just book knowledge about other countries. **Hospitality gives the possibility of making a journey without leaving home** - similarly as in a journey we have the opportunity to meet another human being and God in another human being. It is not acquiring information, it is experiencing another human being.

Reversal of logic
=================

The animator starts with a question:

- What can block us in being hospitable?

Listen to the answers. If an answer regarding lack of resources (we have nothing to share) does not fall - suggest such an answer asking if it can block us.

**Summary:**

In Sukkot we are in tents, but it is a feast of abundance. We feast, even though we are away from home. In Christianity, we invite children to the Eucharist - to participate in the mystery. One does not need to wait until we are fully ready, until we have a fully furnished apartment. We can and should abound already now.
