Introduction for the animator
==============================

The content of this meeting is crucial for the goals of the further substantive content of the retreat. Properly conducting the meeting will enable participants to enter the retreat immediately - they will be forced to think. I suggest carefully analyzing the outline in terms of your group - whether some questions might prove too difficult, maybe something is too trivial - here I count on a kind of "animator instinct". Conducting the meeting "on the fly" without analyzing the outline can be quite problematic, hence good preparation is the key to leading the meeting. Two important fragments from the Bible have been included - I suggest praying them during the Tent of Meeting. This stems from the fact that the words we speak must be alive, it must be noticeable that what we are talking about is our way of life or the goal we are heading towards. At the same time, one should not be afraid of expressing one's weaker sides - I advise avoiding the attitude of the "ideal animator". The animator's attitude is the basic factor in opening up the group.

Prayer
======

The animator must know how long the meeting can last - depending on this, they must assess the length of prayer, because there isn't much time ;))

Getting acquainted
===================

I leave this to the animator's discretion, it can be based on introductory questions, or it can be some kind of game. Here you can also present the rules of working in groups and organizational matters of this type.

Proper content
==============

GOAL: Proper understanding of the word freedom and awakening the desire to strive for true freedom, which can be achieved through truth, and that is in Christ.

* How would you define the word freedom?

Definitions of freedom:

   1. Freedom is when no one commands and no one forbids

   2. Lack of coercion, a situation in which one can make choices from among all available options.

   3. Freedom is the source of human dignity. Only it constitutes the basic reason for self-respect.

   4. Freedom does not mean freedom from all guiding principles. Freedom is freedom for development consistent with the laws of the structure of human existence. (...) It means obedience to principles that govern optimal individual development.

   5. Freedom is the possibility, rooted in reason and will, of acting or not acting, doing this or something else, thus undertaking voluntary actions by oneself.

   6. Freedom is a right permitted by law.

   7. Freedom is the core of the inalienable dignity of every human being, as it constitutes their deepest "I".

   8. Freedom is the possibility, rooted in reason and will, of acting or not acting, doing this or something else, thus undertaking voluntary actions by oneself.

* Which of the definitions could you most identify with? (Here we lay out the cut-out definitions of freedom and encourage making a choice.)


Submitting for discussion the statement: "freedom from... (lack of principles) vs. freedom for a certain choice" (here the animator must think about the exact content of this message depending on the group's predispositions - especially age)

* What do you understand by this?

   * Freedom to choose a school, freedom to choose company, freedom to choose a wife (you can humorously refer to Mormons where the choice is made independently at a stadium. The husband sees his wife for the first time in that place.)

   * Freedom from school, freedom from mom's commands, freedom from going to church (often going to church is a command from parents - we need to look at it in this light!),

* Do you feel free in both definitions of freedom?

I think you will agree that often our decisions are guided, to tell the truth, by an external factor.

* What do you think - what can influence the decisions we make? Parents, television, or maybe something else? (you can emphasize the problem of mass media and manipulation a bit here, but you don't need to elaborate, because there will probably be one conference on this topic)

.. note:: Participants have more or less familiarized themselves with the preliminary definition, now we need to show the more Christological sense of this definition.

Statement for discussion: "Christianity - source of freedom, or limitation?" (Here we allow loose digressions. If the animator conducts this point well, an interesting discussion may arise, but we must remember that time is pressing.)

When participants have said something, you can try to "corner" them a bit (here depending on how confident the animator feels) with statements (primarily forcing them to think - learning to reject schemas, an attempt to break the direction of thinking "I say so, because that's the key"):

* What kind of freedom is it that limits?
* How can a tangle of prohibitions and commands present in Christianity be called freedom?
* Why is the free choice of, for example, a life full of sex, alcohol, etc., supposedly enslavement, while choosing Jesus is already freedom?
* Doesn't faith in Jesus work on the principle of addiction?
* Isn't that the point, to "addict" one's life to Jesus?
* Why then should this addiction be freedom, and others not?

.. note:: Here the animator has a chance to prove themselves. Most importantly, it must be visible that the content the animator conveys flows from their life, that they live by it!

* Do you find answers to the previously asked questions in this fragment? (This part may be relatively difficult and may require reading twice and help in the form of hints from the animator.)

Wouldn't their words go in one ear and out the other?" -> analogy to the fragment, similarly must have felt those who listened to Jesus' teaching. It is not easy teaching and simple to accept! * Why did Jesus tell the Jews that they were living in slavery? After all, no one was holding them in chains, they could do what they wanted? (->we should arrive at the answer that it's about sin.)

* Why should sin cause slavery? Let's say I sin - I don't know... I snap at my mom, I hit a friend - after all, nothing will happen to me then, the next day no one remembers it.

* What does it mean that Truth sets free? What is this truth? What does this liberation consist of? (hints for the animator - addition: Truth that liberates.)

"If I tell the truth, why do you not believe me?" – we often show disapproval of Jesus' words, we deny His teaching by saying it directly or simply by sinning.

We need to make the group aware that everyone faces a clear choice - God wants us to make such decisions. Conclusions
===========

If time allows, we can briefly recall the essence of the meeting:

What freedom is - how it differs from the one we can superficially know, how to achieve it (in God, in Truth), the effort (not to give up) freedom is not enslavement (this sounds relatively strange, but it has its meaning), but is our good and the path to true joy.

Application
===========

As an application, let each participant consider whether they are ready today to make a decision to strive for true freedom. Whether they are ready to undertake the effort of fighting for it. Let them "bring" this decision with them to evening prayer.
